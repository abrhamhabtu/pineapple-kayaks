Rent the kayak, or take the tour?

Every Secret Falls day on Kauaʻi is the same two moves: paddle about two miles up the Wailua, then hike a muddy mile to Uluwehi. Guided companies and self-guided rentals use that route. The fork is who owns the clock, and what you pay for it.

What a guided tour buys you

A van, a launch time, a guide in the water, and usually lunch. Fine if you do not want to think. The cost is $100–$300 a person, a group pace, and a hard out from the pool so the van can run the next trip.

What a self-guided rental buys you

The boat, the gear, the map, and the day. We load your car in Līhuʻe. You drive five minutes to the Wailua, paddle when you are ready, and swim as long as you want. From $45, or $40 in a group of 4+. Beginners do this every week — both rivers are flat water.

You do need someone comfortable driving with a kayak on the roof, or you book delivery. You also need shoes that stay on in mud. That is the whole extra list.

Who should rent

  • Anyone who has sat in a kayak once, or is willing to learn on flat water
  • Families who want the kids to swim without a whistle
  • Couples who would rather eat their own lunch at the falls
  • Groups of 4+ who would rather pay $40 than $150 each

Who should tour

Nobody in the car wants a boat on the roof, and you do not want delivery either. You want a cultural talk the whole paddle. Or you are solo and would rather follow a group than read a map. Fair. That is what those companies are for.

If the only objection is “we have never kayaked” — Hanalei is the easier first day, then Secret Falls on a weekday. We would rather send you out than sell you a packed tour.
